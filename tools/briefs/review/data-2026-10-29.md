## BUG

**B1. The Reading section corrupts every decimal number: "51. 8%", "48. | 2%".**
`docs/app.js:1220-1229` (`paragraphs`) cuts the reading into sentences with `/[^.!?]+(?:[.!?]+...)/g`, which treats the
point of "51.8%" as a full stop, then re-joins the pieces with a space, and groups three "sentences" per paragraph, so the
paragraph break can land inside the number. Reproduced on `#/dossier/BRA` (English): "Lula won the runoff with about 51. 8% to
Flavio Bolsonaro's 48." and the next paragraph begins "2%, reversing...". Also `#/dossier/COL` ("a 49." / "66% mandate").
33 of the 205 readings contain decimals (44 numbers): AGO ARG AUS AUT BDI BEL BRA CHL CHN COL CYP CZE DZA ESP EST FIN FSM GEO
HRV IND ISR LVA MAR MNG NLD NOR PER PHL RUS SGP SWE THA org-nato; in 14 places (12 readings) the paragraph break itself falls
inside the number (AGO ARG AUT x2 BDI BRA COL ESP FIN MAR PER SWE x2 org-nato). Change: split on `/(?<=[.!?])\s+(?=[A-Z"'(])/` (point followed by
space and a capital) instead of at every point.

**B2. No reading is ever shown in Italian.**
The same function, `docs/app.js:1220-1229` and its call at `:1271`, translates through `esc()` (`:1210`, which calls `T()` on the
whole string it gets) only after re-cutting the English reading into three-sentence paragraphs, so the fingerprint of each
paragraph matches nothing. All 205 readings are translated (`i18n.py status`: cards 3352/3352), none appears. Reproduced:
`?lang=it#/dossier/SRB` shows Italian objectives and English "Serbia runs a hedging strategy with one fixed point..." in
the four `p.reading` blocks. Change: `paragraphs(T(gs.reading))` (translate the whole block, then split). Note for when it is
fixed: the Italian COL reading is truncated (881 characters for 2,030 in English: the "Added on 16 Oct 2026..." paragraph
is missing), see I14.

**B3. Thirty-five organisations sit at coordinates [0, 0] and are drawn in the Gulf of Guinea.**
`tools/datasets/wikidata.py:205-206` creates every org stub with `"coords": [0, 0], "seat": ""`; the 35 files
`data/actors/org-*.yaml` (org-un, org-asean, org-gcc, org-opec, org-brics, org-au, org-aes, org-ecowas, org-mercosur,
org-csto, org-eaeu, org-quad, org-g7, org-g20, org-wto, org-oecd, org-sco, ...) were never given a seat.
`docs/app.js:502-504` draws every non-state actor the point of view grades as a square at `proj(a.coords)`, and `:513-520`
labels it. Reproduced: `#/view/KWT` has squares and labels "GULF COOPERATION COUNCIL" and "OPEC" at x=339.5, y=350 (the same
pixel, labels stacked). 43 of the 205 full cards grade at least one such org (NGA six; org-un 12 cards, org-asean 8, org-gcc 6,
org-aes 5, org-brics 5). Change: give them a real seat (the headquarters) or skip `coords == [0,0]` at `app.js:502`.

**B4. Nine organisation pages link "Wikipedia ↗" to the wrong thing.**
The `wikipedia:` field of nine org stubs holds a wrong Wikidata item (the stubs were written once from ids typed by hand
and never regenerated; the later-corrected `ORGS` table in `wikidata.py` and `org-ids.json` agree with each other, not with
the files). Labels looked up on the Wikidata API: org-brics -> Q47539 "South Atlantic Peace and Cooperation Zone"; org-gcc ->
Q170989 "Lorolwana" (a village in Botswana); org-g7 -> Q170481 "Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation"; org-g20 -> Q19097 "sect";
org-oas -> Q7814 "Giotto"; org-nam -> Q152776 "Audrey Munson"; org-csto -> Q190185 "Zentralarchiv zur Erforschung der Geschichte der
Juden in Deutschland"; org-sco -> Q190364 "Zentrale fuer Einkauf und Service"; org-aes -> Q122258006 (a paper on electrotechnics).
Reproduced on `#/dossier/org-gcc` (the link text says Wikipedia, the target is Q170989, while the source list on the same page
cites the right Q217172). The other 23 org ids are correct. This is exactly the lesson in CLAUDE.md ("never type a Wikidata id").
Change: write the ids from `data/datasets/wikidata/org-ids.json` into the nine files (and see I7).

**B5. The Currents page says "No grade or trend changed since the last build" after any second build of the day.**
`tools/build.py:678-681` takes `previous = sorted(SNAP.glob("*.json"))[-1]`, which after the first build of a day is today's own
snapshot (written at `:692`), so `changes` compares today with today. Reproduced: `#/currents` shows "Since 2026-10-29: No grade or
trend changed since the last build": the comparison can never show a change made earlier the same day (cards were updated twice
on 29 October, commits 0de13c94 and fd693ea8, and rebuilt in between). `changes_week` (Watch page) is unaffected. Change: `previous = [p for p in ... if p.stem < today.isoformat()]`.

## INCONSISTENCY

**I1. `reciprocate.py` and `build.py` mirror alliances differently: 57 written stances read "partner" where the build's rule gives "ally".**
`tools/reciprocate.py:25` maps ally and reluctant ally to partner without the exception added to the build on 2026-10-16
("ally comes back as ally where a treaty binds", `build.py:369-376`). 57 stances in `data/stances` carry `reciprocal_of` with
grade `partner` between co-members of NATO/EU/CSTO, so Germany's map reads Finland, Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Estonia, Latvia,
Lithuania, Italy, the Netherlands, Belgium, Portugal, Greece, Canada, the UK, Croatia, Slovakia as "partner" (16 pairs on DEU
alone) while those cards read Germany "ally"; same for ISL/CAN->DNK, PRT->ESP, LTU->FIN, DNK/FIN/ROU/NLD/CYP->FRA, etc. (confidence
C, flagged "mirrored", so seams and clients ignore them, but the map shows them). Change: give `reciprocate.py` the same treaty
exception (or delete those 57 so the dataset floor "ally" applies) and rerun.

**I2. The build is not deterministic: every rebuild dirties 41 `docs/actors/*.json` and `docs/data.json`.**
`tools/build.py:342,345,347` iterate Python sets (`members_of`), so the order of the derived ally stances follows
`PYTHONHASHSEED`. Verified: seed 1 twice gives identical bytes, seed 2 gives a different `data.json` (md5 7e05... vs a1c2...). After my
one run `git status` showed 43 modified files (content identical as a multiset; only stance order and the gzip byte count in
`stats.payload` differ). The brief says the build "rewrites `docs/data.json` identically": it does not. Change: `sorted(members_of[org])`
in both loops. (The `?v=` stamp in `index.html` also changes on every build, by design, `build.py:948`.)

**I3. Denmark has no row in the Wikidata states file, so it has no borders, no weight and no Italian name.**
`data/datasets/wikidata/states.json` has 196 states; the 197th state actor, DNK (a full card), is missing (cause not checked;
`wikidata.py:146` adds only its memberships by hand). Consequences: `borders.json` has no DNK entry and Germany's
neighbour list omits Denmark (DEU: AUT BEL CHE CZE FRA GBR LUX NLD POL SWE), so DNK has no row in Encirclement and is never
flagged as a gap; `worldbank.py:71` drops any iso not in `states.json`, so DNK weighs 0 in gatherings, flanks and levers
(TWN and VAT are also weightless: TWN is documented, VAT trivial); `names.json` has no "Danimarca". Change: add DNK to
`states.json` by hand in `wikidata.py` (as for Kosovo), rerun borders, worldbank and names.

**I4. CLAUDE.md promises a check that does not exist: "the build warns about `watch` dates that have passed".**
Update cycle step 2 depends on it. `grep watch tools/build.py` finds nothing; no actor file has a `watch` field (the word occurs
only as prose, "What to watch", in readings; JOURNAL.md:59 lists "the watch dates" among ideas left for later). The cards that
should go first in a cycle (elections, mandates, rulings) cannot be found by the build. Change: either implement it or
amend step 2.

**I5. GDELT: Timor-Leste is lost under another code, five more states have no row, and a failed run wipes the file.**
`heat.json` has 191 actors; no row for BIH, MNE, ROU, SVN, TLS, XKX. For TLS the cause is visible: GDELT's code for East Timor,
`TMP`, is not mapped and its 116 events are counted under `not_actors` (`tools/datasets/gdelt.py`, no CAMEO-to-atlas mapping). For
the other five I found zero events under ROM/ROU/SVN/MNE/BIH/KSV in three raw days I sampled (cause unknown). The Watch page
therefore never shows them and does not say so. Also `gdelt.py` writes `heat.json` and `heat-<date>.json` even when `done == 0`
(every download failed), which replaces a good file with an empty one. Change: map TMP -> TLS; list the blind states on the Watch page; skip the write when `done == 0`.

**I6. The dates on the Data page are file modification times.**
`tools/build.py:859-860` (`fdate`) uses `st_mtime` for the six dataset rows (e.g. "Borders 2026-10-16", "UNGA votes 2026-10-12"). A fresh
clone, or a copy to the other PC, makes every dataset "fetched" on the day of the clone. Change: read the date from the dataset's own
`source.fetched` field (borders, worldbank, gdelt already have it).
